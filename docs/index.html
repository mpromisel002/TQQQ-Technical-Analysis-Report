    Support levels in detail

    LevelZone priceZone range From priceTimes heldLast tested StrengthStatus
    Zone price
    The middle of the band — the single number to watch, weighted towards the days with the most trading.
    Zone range
    The lowest and highest price inside the band. Support is an area, not an exact number.
    From price
    How far the zone price is from the last close: negative below it, positive above it.
    Times held
    How many separate swing lows formed in that band during the window.
    Strength
    A 0–100 score: how often price turned there (40%), how recently (30%), how much volume traded at those turns (20%) and how far price bounced afterwards (10%). Scores only compare levels inside the window you picked. 70+ is Strong, 40–69 Moderate, below 40 Weak.
    Status
    In play — price is inside the zone now. Recently tested — price visited it in the last 10 sessions. Holding — untouched recently. Above price — the price has closed below the zone, with the date it did so.

    Selling covered calls

    StrikePremium you getRise to strike Chance of being calledBreakeven Return if calledTrading

    Strike
    Under each strike, why it is listed. At A1, A2… — the first listed strike at or above that level above the price. 30% pick, 20% pick — the strike whose chance of being called is nearest that figure, for sellers who choose by how likely the shares are to be called away.
    Premium
    The bid, not the last trade or the middle price: what a seller is likely to get. Beside it, as a % of the last close; under it, that % × 365 ÷ days to expiry, "a year". The yearly figure is for comparing expiries, not a forecast: selling a call every week will not add up to it.
    Rise to strike
    How far the price must rise to reach the strike, and how many typical days of movement (ATR) that is.
    Chance of being called
    The chance the price ends above the strike at expiry, worked out from the option's own price (its implied volatility). It is the market's figure, not a forecast.
    Breakeven
    The last close minus the premium. Below it, the shares and the call together lose money.
    Return if called
    The rise to the strike plus the premium, as a % of the last close. Nothing above the strike is kept.

        Method, definitions & data

        Plain-English glossary

        Support
        A price area where buyers have repeatedly stepped in and stopped a fall. It is evidence of past behaviour, not a guarantee about the future.
        Swing low
        A day that is lower than the 3 days before it and the 3 days after it — a local bottom on the chart.
        Window
        How much history the method looks at. A 12-month window finds long-standing levels; a 1-month window finds only what matters right now.
        Zone
        Several swing lows at nearly the same price, merged into one band. Real support is a range, not a single number.
        Touch
        One swing low inside a zone. More touches means more buyers have defended that area.
        Level above price
        A zone the price has since closed clearly below, labelled A1, A2 and so on. It no longer counts as support, but it stays on the chart and in the table because rises often stall where an old floor gave way.
        ATR
        Average True Range: the size of a typical day's move. Used here to decide how wide a support zone should be and how far away a stop would sit.
        RSI
        A 0–100 momentum gauge. Above 70 the recent rise looks stretched; below 30 the recent fall looks stretched.
        MACD
        Compares a fast and a slow average of price to show whether momentum is building or fading.
        Moving average
        The average closing price over the last 20, 50 or 200 days — a smoothed picture of the trend.

        How support is found

        1. Swing lows. A day is a swing low when its low is the lowest from 3 sessions before it to 3 sessions after it. The most recent 3 sessions can't be confirmed yet and are drawn as hollow “pending” markers.
        2. Rolling low. The lowest low of the last 3 sessions is drawn as a step line — the nearest short-term floor.
        3. Zones. Swing lows within a set distance of each other (default 0.5 × the 14-day ATR) are merged into one zone. The zone price is the volume-weighted average of its lows; the range runs from its lowest to highest low.
        4. Strength. Touches 40%, recency 30%, volume at the touches 20%, bounce afterwards 10%. Every zone found is listed: below the price as S1, S2, … (nearest first) and above it as A1, A2, ….
        5. Levels above price. Once price closes more than half the cluster distance below a zone, that zone moves from the support list to the levels above the price (A1 is the nearest). The break is also reported under “What changed”.

        Trend call

        Three checks: price above/below both the 50 and 200-day averages; the 50-day average's slope over 20 sessions; and whether the last two swing lows are rising or falling. All three agree → Uptrend or Downtrend; otherwise Mixed.
