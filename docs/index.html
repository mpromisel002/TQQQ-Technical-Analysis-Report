Price & support zones

Each green band is a price area where TQQQ has stopped falling and turned back up. The darker the band, the more reliable the level has been. S1 is the closest support below today's price, S2 the next one down, and so on.

          Method, definitions & data

          Plain-English glossary

          Support
          A price area where buyers have repeatedly stepped in and stopped a fall. It is evidence of past behaviour, not a guarantee about the future.
          Swing low
          A day that is lower than the 3 days before it and the 3 days after it — a local bottom on the chart.
          Window
          How much history the method looks at. A 12-month window finds long-standing levels; a 1-month window finds only what matters right now.
          Zone
          Several swing lows at nearly the same price, merged into one band. Real support is a range, not a single number.
          Touch
          One swing low inside a zone. More touches means more buyers have defended that area.
          Broken level
          Price closed clearly below a zone. The level stops counting as support and leaves the list.
          ATR
          Average True Range: the size of a typical day's move. Used here to decide how wide a support zone should be and how far away a stop would sit.
          RSI
          A 0–100 momentum gauge. Above 70 the recent rise looks stretched; below 30 the recent fall looks stretched.
          MACD
          Compares a fast and a slow average of price to show whether momentum is building or fading.
          Moving average
          The average closing price over the last 20, 50 or 200 days — a smoothed picture of the trend.

          How support is found

          1. Swing lows. A day is a swing low when its low is the lowest from 3 sessions before it to 3 sessions after it. The most recent 3 sessions can't be confirmed yet and are drawn as hollow “pending” markers.
          2. Rolling low. The lowest low of the last 3 sessions is drawn as a step line — the nearest short-term floor.
          3. Zones. Swing lows within a set distance of each other (default 0.5 × the 14-day ATR, about 2–3% for TQQQ) are merged into one zone. The zone price is the volume-weighted average of its lows; the range runs from its lowest to highest low.
          4. Strength. Touches 40%, recency 30%, volume at the touches 20%, bounce afterwards 10%. The five strongest zones are reported.
          5. Broken zones. Once price closes more than half the cluster distance below a zone, that zone is dropped from the list; the break is reported under “What changed”.

          Trend call

          Three checks: price above/below both the 50 and 200-day averages; the 50-day average's slope over 20 sessions; and whether the last two swing lows are rising or falling. All three agree → Uptrend or Downtrend; otherwise Mixed.

          Data

          Daily prices for TQQQ and QQQ, adjusted for splits and dividends, from Yahoo Finance (with Tiingo / Alpha Vantage as backups). About two years are loaded so the 200-day average is ready across the full 12-month view. A GitHub Actions job refreshes the data every weekday evening after the US close, checks for missing sessions, zero volume and suspicious jumps, and only then publishes. Current settings are saved in the page address so views can be bookmarked or shared.

          Educational use only — not investment advice. TQQQ is a 3× leveraged ETF that resets daily; it can lose value quickly, and over periods longer than a day its return can differ greatly from 3× the Nasdaq-100. Support levels are descriptions of past trading, not predictions, and levels in leveraged funds can break sharply. Data may be delayed or contain errors.
